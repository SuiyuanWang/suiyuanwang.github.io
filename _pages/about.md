  <h1>Suiyuan Wang</h1>
  <p class="research-statement">My research combines remote sensing, geospatial AI, and spatiotemporal analysis to measure urban change and inform sustainable neighborhood development.</p>
  <p>I am a Ph.D. candidate in Geography at the University at Buffalo, advised by Dr. Le Wang. My dissertation examines high spatiotemporal resolution impervious-surface change using ensemble remote sensing foundation models. My broader interests include fine-resolution population estimation and the social and environmental dimensions of urban change.</p>

<h2 id="research">Research highlights</h2>
<section class="research-feature" aria-labelledby="urban-change-title">
  <h3 id="urban-change-title">Monitoring urban change with remote sensing</h3>
  <p>How can we monitor changes in urban impervious surfaces at fine spatial and temporal scales? My work uses PlanetScope image time series to quantify change, alongside ongoing research on ensemble remote sensing foundation models for nationwide U.S. mapping.</p>

    <ol class="research-workflow" aria-label="Conceptual overview of the urban change research">

      <li><span class="workflow-step">03</span><strong>Understand</strong><span>Impervious-surface change and urban development</span></li>

<section class="research-feature" aria-labelledby="population-title">
  <h3 id="population-title">Mapping fine-resolution populations</h3>
  <p>How can population maps reflect changes within neighborhoods over time? In work with Le Wang, I combine remote sensing, mobile-phone data, and land-use information to estimate monthly populations at the census-block level.</p>
  <figure class="population-figure">
    <a href="{{ '/images/remotesensing.0227.fig.001.jpg' | relative_url }}" aria-label="View the population-mapping workflow at full size">
      <img src="{{ '/images/remotesensing.0227.fig.001.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Two parallel modeling steps estimate monthly census-block-group populations and block-level weights; dasymetric allocation combines them to estimate monthly census-block populations." width="660" height="609" loading="lazy">
    </a>
    <figcaption>Population-mapping framework from Wang &amp; Wang (2024). Monthly population estimates for census block groups are combined with block-level weights to produce finer-resolution population maps. Select the figure to view it at full size.</figcaption>
  </figure>
  <p class="research-output"><a href="https://doi.org/10.34133/remotesensing.0227">Read the open-access paper</a></p>
</section>
